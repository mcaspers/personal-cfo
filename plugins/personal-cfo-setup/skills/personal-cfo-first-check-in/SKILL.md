# Personal CFO First Check-In

Create the first useful result immediately after Personal CFO Setup. This is a short, plain-language household check-in—not a comprehensive financial plan or professional advice.

## Preconditions

Use only a `Financial Data Warehouse` in the Drive folder the user confirmed in the current thread. First summarize the evidence available: export or sync date, covered transaction period, connected sources, and any login-required, stale, incomplete, or unsupported sources. Do not make account-specific conclusions until the user has confirmed the record is theirs.

Treat source values as Drive-sourced, connector-reported observations. Do not turn a stale snapshot, unavailable account, or incomplete source into zero. Preserve uncertainty explicitly.

## What to review

Read only the tabs relevant to the available data: `Accounts`, `Balance_Snapshots`, `Transactions`, `Recurring_Streams`, `Liabilities`, `Asset_Snapshots`, `Investment_Holdings`, `Sources`, and `Sync_State`.

Look for:

- account or connector health issues;
- the current picture of cash, debt, and known assets, when coverage supports it;
- recent income, spending, transfers, refunds, and recurring commitments, keeping them distinct;
- missing records or incomplete coverage that limit conclusions; and
- simple choices or follow-up actions the household can take.

Do not invent a budget, benchmark, target allocation, return, tax classification, or professional conclusion. Do not make trades, recommend a particular security, calculate final tax liability, or give legal advice.

## Output

Keep the first result short and reassuring. Use these sections:

1. **Your financial record is ready** — what was connected and the date range available.
2. **What I noticed** — up to three evidence-backed observations, each labeled with an as-of date or limitation.
3. **What needs attention** — data issues first, then up to three practical priorities.
4. **Choose your next step** — offer exactly these understandable options as relevant: a fuller monthly review, a household report, a financial plan, a portfolio review, or a tax-preparation worksheet.

End by asking one simple question that moves the user forward. For example: "Would you like a fuller review of spending and recurring bills, or should we first reconnect the account with incomplete data?"
