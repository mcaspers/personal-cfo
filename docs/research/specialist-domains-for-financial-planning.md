# Research: Specialist domains for Personal CFO Financial Plan

**Date:** 2026-09-10  
**Scope:** A recommended U.S.-focused taxonomy for specialist planning modules, using CFP Board and U.S. government/regulator sources. This is product-design research, not financial, tax, legal, or insurance advice.

## Finding

Yes: **Cash Flow Strategy** is a real, valuable specialist domain. It is broader than a budget: it turns observed income timing, fixed commitments, variable spending, savings transfers, debt minimums, and irregular expenses into an actionable short- and medium-term liquidity plan. CFP Board explicitly includes income, expenses, cash flow, savings, liquidity, assets, liabilities, taxes, benefits, insurance, estate plans, and retirement accounts among the information relevant to financial planning. Its worked example treats cash reserves, recurring commitments, and unallocated annual cash flow as inputs to choices across multiple goals. [CFP Board: Guide to the 7-Step Financial Planning Process](https://www.cfp.net/-/media/files/cfp-board/standards-and-ethics/compliance-resources/guide-to-financial-planning-process.pdf?hash=A8F02CC2451BE07E4FB05DE009A64F68&la=en)

The CFPB's cash-flow budget tool likewise starts with tracking income, resources, and expenses, including everyday spending, bills, and savings; that supports giving Cash Flow Strategy its own planning seam rather than treating it as a one-time spending report. [CFPB: Creating a Cash Flow Budget Tool](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_your-money-your-goals_cash_flow_budget_tool_2018-11_ADA.pdf)

## Recommended taxonomy

The parent **Financial Plan** owns objectives, prioritization, cross-domain tradeoffs, the durable plan, and the final user-facing synthesis. It asks specialists for a bounded brief; specialists do not independently alter household priorities or recommend/execute products or transactions.

| Specialist | Owns | Minimum useful output | Keep out of scope / escalate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cash Flow Strategy** (first specialist) | Income and bill timing; recurring versus discretionary spending; irregular/seasonal expenses; liquidity buffer; unallocated cash; funding sequence for user-selected goals. | Evidence-dated cash-flow baseline; next 30/90-day pinch points; confirmed planning assumptions; 2–3 funding scenarios; tradeoffs; a user-selected spending/savings cadence. | No silent budget creation, debt settlement, transfer execution, or claims that a cash reserve is “adequate.” Escalate imminent inability to pay housing or other critical bills to the creditor/servicer and appropriate nonprofit or HUD-approved counseling resources. CFPB directs borrowers worried about a mortgage payment to contact their servicer promptly and a HUD-approved counselor. [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/if-i-cant-pay-my-mortgage-loan-what-are-my-options-en-268/) |
| **Tax Strategy & Estimated Payments** | Calendar of known taxable events; source-dated income/withholding/payments; illustrative estimated-tax and cash-reserve scenarios; questions for a CPA/EA. Useful for self-employment, investments, equity compensation, rental income, and large liquidity events. | Tax-event ledger; explicit assumptions; scenario comparison; payment/filing checklist; unresolved treatment questions; auditable record of source dates and user decisions. | Never calculate a final liability, certify a tax position/deduction, prepare/file a return, or tell the user how to conceal ownership or income. Form 1040-ES is the IRS mechanism for income not subject to withholding; the 2026 instructions describe a required annual payment calculation for avoiding underpayment penalties. [IRS: Form 1040-ES](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1040-es) [2026 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f1040es.pdf) Escalate entity structure, equity events, multistate, foreign, estate/gift, and material tax positions to a CPA, EA, or tax attorney. |
| **Debt & Credit Strategy** | Debt inventory; cash-flow impact; payoff/refinance alternatives; emergency-payment scenarios; user constraints. | Debt schedule with evidence dates; scenario table that separates balance, payoff quote, rate, fees, payment, and timeline; tradeoffs with savings goals. | Do not negotiate debt, direct a specific refinancing product, or treat a displayed mortgage balance as its payoff amount. CFPB notes payoff amounts can include interest/fees and may differ from current balances; compare actual Loan Estimates when evaluating mortgage offers. [CFPB: payoff amount](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-payoff-amount-and-is-it-the-same-as-my-current-balance-en-205/) [CFPB: compare offers](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/compare/) |
| **Benefits & Retirement** | Employer-plan and benefit facts, contribution/match/vesting questions, retirement-income assumptions, and plan-document gaps. | Benefits/retirement fact sheet; missing documents; contribution and liquidity scenarios supplied to the parent; professional questions. | No security selection, allocation recommendation, trade instruction, valuation, or assertion that a retirement outcome is sufficient. DOL distinguishes defined-benefit and defined-contribution plans and documents participant rights/information under ERISA. [DOL: retirement-plan FAQ](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/faqs/retirement-plans-and-erisa) Escalate investment recommendations to an appropriate qualified professional. The SEC describes advisers as providing investment advice about securities and notes their best-interest duty. [Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/working-investment-professional/investment-advisers) |
| **Equity Compensation** (conditional) | RSU, option, ESPP, ESOP, and private-equity event calendar; vest/exercise/sale fact inventory; concentration and liquidity/tax-reserve scenarios. | Event timeline; source-dated plan terms; alternatives that feed Cash Flow and Tax Strategy; questions for a CPA/EA and investment professional. | Do not tell the user whether to exercise, hold, sell, or diversify, nor determine tax basis or tax treatment. IRS guidance treats employer-provided stock and other compensation as fact-specific income/tax topics. [IRS Publication 525](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p525) |
| **Risk, Insurance & Resilience** | Coverage/document inventory; premiums/deductibles; renewal dates; downside exposure questions; impact of a loss on plan cash flow. | Coverage inventory with evidence dates; gaps/unknowns; stress scenarios; questions for the insurer or licensed agent. | No recommendation of a policy, coverage limit, insurer, or suitability. State rules and circumstances matter. NAIC describes insurance as protection against the cost of disasters and emergencies and points consumers to their state insurance department; it also recommends annual home-policy review as circumstances/replacement cost change. [NAIC](https://content.naic.org/consumer) [NAIC: homeowners](https://content.naic.org/consumer/homeowners-insurance.htm) |
| **Estate & Household Continuity** | Document and beneficiary inventory; ownership/beneficiary questions; incapacity/continuity checklist; records needed for an attorney. | Dated document inventory; missing/unknown items; household questions and attorney briefing checklist. | Never draft legal documents, determine legal validity, advise on asset protection or ownership concealment, or choose a trust/entity structure. Refer all legal/estate choices to a licensed attorney in the relevant state. |
| **Property & Major Purchases** (conditional) | Home/vehicle carrying costs, purchase affordability scenarios, and document readiness. | Evidence-based carrying-cost scenario; cash-flow and goal tradeoffs; decision checklist; lender/CPA/attorney questions where applicable. | No appraisal or mortgage/loan product recommendation. CFPB recommends comparing actual Loan Estimates from multiple lenders for home-loan decisions. [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/compare/) |
| **Business-owner Finance** (conditional) | Separate household and business cash flow; owner-pay, tax-reserve, debt-guarantee, and benefit/retirement-plan facts. | Linked-but-separate cash-flow view; obligations/trigger calendar; scenarios for the parent; CPA/attorney questions. | No entity formation, tax conclusion, valuation, payroll, or transaction advice. Escalate those decisions to a CPA/EA, business attorney, and where applicable a valuation or transaction professional. |

## Recommended release order

1. **Cash Flow Strategy** — broad value for every household, uses existing synced transaction data, and creates the planning substrate for every other specialist.
2. **Tax Strategy & Estimated Payments** — the clearest expressed demand, but build it as an auditable scenario and professional-handoff module, not a filing engine.
3. **Debt & Credit Strategy** — combines naturally with cash flow and supports clear alternative modeling.
4. **Benefits & Retirement** — particularly valuable for employer-plan facts; preserve a bright line against securities advice.
5. **Risk, Insurance & Resilience** and **Estate & Household Continuity** — high-value gap detection and document organization, but inherently dependent on licensed-professional decisions.
6. **Equity Compensation**, **Property & Major Purchases**, and **Business-owner Finance** — activate only when the household has those facts; keep security, tax, lending, and entity choices within specialist professional-handoff boundaries.

This ordering deliberately avoids a generic “investment strategist.” The existing parent can model user-selected savings assumptions; a specialist that becomes individualized security selection or trade direction would cross into a higher-risk advisory area. Cash-flow, debt, and tax-event planning instead create a useful sequence: **Can the household fund the decision? What does it change? What must a qualified professional confirm?**

## Shared specialist contract

Every specialist should return the same machine- and human-readable brief to Financial Plan:

1. **Scope and decision question** — user-confirmed goal, time horizon, constraints, and what the module will not decide.
2. **Evidence** — sources and effective dates, plus known coverage gaps.
3. **Facts, assumptions, and unknowns** — never blended.
4. **Alternatives and tradeoffs** — illustrative, sensitivity-aware results; no promised outcome.
5. **Escalation questions** — named questions for a CPA/EA, attorney, licensed insurance agent, lender, or investment professional.
6. **User decision and next review trigger** — the parent alone incorporates a confirmed decision into the durable household plan.

Tax must be especially constrained: the IRS says “practice before the IRS” includes preparing or filing documents, communications with the IRS, and written tax advice concerning arrangements with potential tax avoidance/evasion. The product should therefore organize facts and produce scenarios/questions, never present itself as tax representation or professional written advice. [IRS: practice before the IRS](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/frequently-asked-questions)

## Product implication

Call the first module **Cash Flow Strategy**, not “Cash Flow Strategist.” The former names a concrete planning capability; the latter implies a standing professional role. In the user experience, keep it simple: “Let’s make a plan for where the next dollars go and when they need to be available.” It should complement—not replace—the Financial Plan’s optional budget. A budget is a confirmed target; Cash Flow Strategy is the analysis and cadence that makes that target workable.
